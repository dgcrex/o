## 1) EXECUTIVE SUMMARY OF THE CORRECTED SEQUENCING

The original checklist (form No Games Ltd unless tax advice supports sole-trader status → secure registered office → verify director/PSC identity → select SIC codes → incorporate → open bank account (gov.uk)) is **mechanically solid but wrongly sequenced** for this project. Three dependencies from the project's own record change the order:

1. **DWP/LCWRA conflict gates Day 1.** You hold an LCWRA award. Becoming a company director and trading can trigger a "gainful self-employment" reassessment under Universal Credit, including the Minimum Income Floor after any start-up period — and undeclared directorship is a compliance risk in itself. This is resolved **before** incorporating, not after. (MIF is usually disapplied in LCWRA cases [3][4][6][7] — but that must be confirmed in writing for this case, not assumed.)
2. **The 29 July incorporation date is premature on address grounds.** The registered office cannot be your home address: the tenancy ends **4 September** with no confirmed accommodation after, so a registered office tied to an address you are about to lose creates a Companies House compliance failure within weeks of formation. A **commercial registered-office/mailbox service in England/Wales** (physical address — not a PO Box, not home [1]) is used from the outset. This also keeps the business address firewalled from live police/tribunal matters, consistent with the compartmentalisation rule already established in this project.
3. **Calendar reality.** The 7-day sprint collides with active deadlines: **bail return 27 August**, the **HMCTS set-aside**, and a tribunal calendar committed to staying clear through **mid-September**. Incorporation and trademark filing can genuinely wait a week; missed tribunal clocks cannot.

Two lateral corrections are also adopted:

- **Funding:** Start Up Loans run credit/affordability checks; on UC/LCWRA with immediate housing instability, a traditional Startup Loan will likely fail underwriting. A **bootstrap/pre-sale model is the primary financial plan**; the loan becomes a later, secondary option once circumstances stabilise.
- **Visa framing:** for "global representations/visas," the Companies House "nature of business" (SIC codes) and public positioning should support an **"extraordinary ability" narrative (e.g., US O-1 visa) from Day 1** — creative/design-led codes, not generic retail only.

**Three-tier classification used throughout (do not flatten):**

| Tier | Items |
|---|---|
| Universal UK legal requirement | Companies House filings, registered office rules, HMRC/tax, consumer law on checkout (pricing, delivery, payment obligation, durable terms — gov.uk), textile fibre-content labels (gov.uk), UK GDPR/ICO, H&S duties, product safety |
| Retailer-entry requirement (JD/Footasylum route) | REACH evidence pack, SMETA/SEDEX audits, GS1 EAN-13 barcodes — **not collectively universal UK legal requirements** (correction accepted), but **mandatory for this project** because the wholesale pitch is a confirmed decision; sequenced after formation and benefits clearance |
| Timing/gating (this project's realities) | LCWRA/UC written advice + DWP disclosure, commercial registered office before 4 Sep, tribunal calendar (27 Aug bail return, HMCTS set-aside, clear to mid-Sep) |

## 9) TRADE-OFFS AND OPEN DECISIONS

**A. Sole trader vs Ltd — and when.**
- *Ltd now (after gates):* limited liability before supplier POs; required credibility for JD/Footasylum wholesale onboarding; clean IP ownership vessel for the trademark; but triggers directorship disclosure (Gate 1), filing obligations, and accounts costs.
- *Sole trader first:* simpler UC reporting, no Companies House exposure while housing is unstable; but weaker for retailer onboarding, personal liability on supplier contracts, and a later conversion re-papers everything (bank, TM ownership, supplier accounts).
- *Recommended posture:* prepare Ltd ready-to-file; decide on the written tax + benefits advice (Gate 1). If advice supports sole-trader status for the interim, trade minimal (pre-sales only) and convert before the wholesale pitch. **Open decision — L5 input required.**

**B. Startup Loan vs bootstrap.**
- Loan: 6% fixed + mentoring, but personal liability and credit/affordability checks likely fail under UC/LCWRA + housing instability — a rejection also sits on file.
- Bootstrap/pre-sale (primary): limited-drop pre-orders fund production; no underwriting; proves demand for any later application. Trade-off: slower, needs compliant pre-order terms (delivery windows disclosed — consumer law).
- *Adopted:* bootstrap primary; loan revisited post-stabilisation. Business plan (pack doc 04) still built loan-grade — it doubles as the JD/Footasylum credibility document.

**C. Visa-oriented SIC framing (US O-1 "extraordinary ability" narrative).**
- SIC codes must be truthful, but chosen to support the narrative: e.g. 74100 (specialised design activities), 90030 (artistic creation), alongside 47910 (internet retail) and 46420 (wholesale of clothing) — design/creative-led, not generic-retail-only. `PLACEHOLDER` — final set at filing.
- Trade-off: SIC codes are descriptive, and O-1 evidence is really built from press, awards, distinctive design output, and commercial traction — so the framing costs nothing but must be backed by a documented body of work from Day 1 (folder 11 collects the owner-role/representation evidence).

**D. How aggressively to front-load trademark/banking/supplier compliance.**
- Front-load: locks the "No Games" mark early (opposition risk falls), bank ready when trading starts, suppliers pre-qualified for the pitch. Cost: fees spent before benefits/housing clarity.
- Defer: cash preserved, but the name is exposed (anyone can file first) and the JD/FA pitch date slips.
- *Adopted middle path:* trademark **search + application prep** now (free/cheap), filing immediately after incorporation; supplier documentation **requests** now (free), POs only after formation; banking pack prepared, submitted post-incorporation.
